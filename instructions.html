A simple Portuguese drilling app

Andrew Brittle, 2026

European Portuguese vocabulary, drilled the same way as the Japanese drill app: pick some words, answer four-option questions or type the answers, and the app keeps a confidence score for every word so the weak ones come round more often.

Getting around

The tabs along the top are in Portuguese - Palavras (words, with Verbs as a pill beside Basics / Travel / Life), Frases (sentences), Números and Gramática - and the page titles are in English. Once you go deeper — into a word list, a grammar note, a drill — a link at the top left says where Back will take you and goes one step back. Your phone's own back gesture or button does the same. On a tab's main page Back does nothing, so you can't leave the app by accident. Back from a drill, or from its results, goes to wherever you started it. The ⚙ button opens Settings, and tapping it again takes you back to where you were. The longer pages also have a Back button at the bottom.

Finding words

Groups are collections that cut across topics: Latest words, Trip essentials (greetings, useful phrases and signs), Eating & drinking out, Getting around town, Core words, and Portugal vs Brazil (every word where Brazil says it differently).

Basics, Travel and Life hold the word lists themselves, split into three so each is a short list. Search looks in the Portuguese, the English and the Brazilian forms, and ignores accents, so nao finds não.

The dice opens the random drills: 20 words from everything, your weakest 20, or 20 you haven't been tested on yet.

Drilling

Tap words to select them, or use Select all, then Drill - the button says how many. Every list - topics and Groups alike - has Select all; lists of 21-30 words also offer Random 10, and bigger ones Random 20. Hold a card to hear it and see any notes; with Tap a card to hear it on, the page becomes a talking phrasebook.

Reverse the drill direction
See the English and pick the Portuguese.
Type the answer instead of choosing
Type the Portuguese yourself. On its own it shows you the English; with Listen on, it's dictation — write down what you hear.
Listen instead of reading
Hear the word instead of seeing it. Needs a Portuguese voice on your phone (see below).
Say it out loud
See the English and say the Portuguese out loud. Tap Show answer (or press Space) to see and hear it, then mark yourself: Got it (1) or Not this time (2). Your mark is scored like any other answer. Also on the Verbs and Grammar screens.

How typed answers are marked

  • Capitals, extra spaces and punctuation never matter.
  • Nouns come with their article (o comboio, a estação) because that's how the gender gets learnt. Leaving the article off still counts, with a reminder. The wrong article (a comboio) is marked wrong.
  • Right letters but wrong or missing accents (voce) counts, with a reminder and half the usual confidence boost — unless you turn on Accents must be right when typing in Settings.
  • Where Brazil uses a different word (o trem for o comboio), the Brazilian form counts, with a note giving the Portuguese one.
  • Entries like obrigado / obrigada accept either form.
  • A typed (or said-out-loud) answer that misses never lowers a word's score; a right one still raises it. Trying the hard way never costs you your greens.

Getting a Portuguese (Portugal) voice

Listening and tap-to-hear use your phone's own text-to-speech. A Brazilian voice sounds very different from Portugal, so the app looks for a Portuguese (Portugal) voice first. On an iPhone or iPad: Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices → Portuguese (Portugal), and download Joana or Catarina. On Android: the Google text-to-speech settings, language Portuguese (Portugal).

Sounds alike

On the dice page. You hear one word of a pair that differ by a single sound - open or closed vowels (avó / avô), nasals (pão / pau), r and rr (caro / carro), nh and lh (sono / sonho) - and pick which it was. The buttons show the English, so you're listening, not reading. Afterwards, tap either button to compare them. With sound off, it shows the English and you pick the spelling. Scored per word, separately from vocab.

Verbs

Under Palavras: tap the Verbs pill. Pick one or more tenses at the top, tap the verbs you want (or Select all, or Random 20, or tap a group heading such as Regular -er to pick the whole group - groups combine), then Drill - up to 20 forms, weakest first. Confidence bars show how you're doing across all verbs and each group (irregular, -ar, -er, -ir) in the tenses you've picked. Each question gives a person and a verb - nós · falar - with the tense and the English, and you pick or type the form. Every person-and-tense combination has its own confidence score, so the forms you get wrong come back more often. Hold a verb to see its full table; tap a row there to hear it.

Numbers

Reading times and Reading numbers make up 10 new questions every round. Times come two ways: everyday, as people say them (São três menos vinte da tarde), and timetable, as stations and opening hours give them (às quinze e quarenta). Both use às / até às / a partir das / por volta das, which is the contractions rule in action. The look-up tools under each show how any number or time is said; tap the answer to hear it. These two have one confidence score each rather than one per question.

Sentences (Frases)

The Sentence builder lays out the parts of a sentence in coloured columns - who, verb, what, where, with whom, when. Tap one card in each column you want; the sentence builds along the top with the English underneath, and each card is said as you tap it. Once you pick a verb, its forms appear under it - for the person in Who (or eu if Who is empty, since Portuguese often leaves it out) and for one that's easily mixed up with it, in the present, past and going-to - and picking the right one is the point. The things you can do with that verb appear in What. Notes point out mismatches, such as a past time with a present verb.

Four modes: Giving (who · verb · what · to whom · when - dou flores à minha mãe; with pedir it's "ask someone for": peço a conta ao empregado) and three more: Doing (who · verb · what · where · with whom · when), Going (who · ir · where to · how · with whom · when - plans use the present, vou amanhã) and Being (who or what · ser or estar · describing word). The When column also has já (already) and ainda não (not yet): they go before the verb, with the past - já comi, ainda não fui. In Being you pick ser (what someone or something is like) or estar (how they are right now), its form, and then the ending that makes the describing word agree: novo, nova, novos, novas.

The Sentence challenge gives you an English sentence to build in Portuguese. Check marks each part right, wrong, missing or extra - and "right verb, check the form" when only the ending is off. Wrong parts come off and you get one more go, then the answer. The challenge keeps a score for each part (Who, Verb, Verb ending, What…), right first time over your last 20 tries, shown on the Sentences page with the part that has most room to grow. It never changes your word scores.

The Reading challenge is the other way round: a Portuguese sentence to read (tap it to hear it), and you pick the English for each part. The verb's forms are shown by meaning - he / she ate, I'm going to eat - so you're reading who and when from the ending. Reading keeps its own part scores. Doing and Going only.

Grammar

Notes on how Portuguese works, each with a drill where one fits. At the bottom, Tables gathers the patterns to glance at: verb endings, the key irregular verbs, contractions, ser and estar, possessives, este / esse / aquele, numbers and the time. The verb, number and time tables are built by the app's own engines, so they always match the drills.

Short rule cards, each with the pattern, the exceptions worth knowing and what Portugal does differently from Brazil. Most end with a drill of just that rule. The contractions drill puts no, na, do, ao, pelo… into real sentences.

On the Sentences page (and on the daily greeting) is a sentence or short exchange, built fresh from the app's people, places, times and verbs, to show the grammar working together. Some are questions about one part of a sentence - when, where, who with, how, what - answered the short way people do (Vai na segunda-feira. / Vai, sim.). Tap a line to hear it; tap ↻ for another.

Desk sheets

On the Sentences page. Three A4 landscape pages with the sentence order across the top and words under each part: pick a word from each column and put the sentence together, so your effort goes on building it rather than finding words. The verb headings show the forms you need (I · he/she · I in the past), the Who cards show the ending that goes with each person, and the When column is grouped by tense so the verb matches. Shuffle gives a different selection; print or save as a PDF. Phones sometimes start the print dialog in portrait: switch it to landscape.

Installing the app

Installed, the app opens full screen from its own icon and works offline. If it's open as a web page, Settings shows an Install the app card with the steps for your device. On iPhone or iPad: Share → Add to Home Screen. On Android: the browser menu (⋮) → Install app (Chrome or Vivaldi). On a computer: the install icon at the right of the address bar in Chrome or Edge.

Your progress

Everything is stored on this device only. Settings has Download backup / Restore from backup to move your scores to another phone.
